SLIDE 8, energy (0:15)
The IEA puts data centres at 415 terawatt hours in 2024, and about 1,200 by 2035, with GPU servers driving the growth.

SLIDE 9, chip vs grid (0:20)
Frontier and LUMI use the same MI250X nodes, and their GFLOPS per watt are almost equal. But LUMI runs on hydropower and heats part of a Finnish town. So a FLOP's carbon cost depends on the grid and heat reuse, not only the chip.

SLIDE 10, Malaysia (0:22)
Johor went from 10 megawatts to over a gigawatt in about three years, and data centres could use almost a third of Peninsular electricity by 2035, on a grid that is 79 percent fossil. One myth check: AI does not emit more CO2 than aviation today. That is a 2030 projection.

SLIDE 11, governance (0:35)
Where the GPUs are decides which country's law covers the data. In data-parallel training, nodes swap gradients every iteration, and gradients can leak training samples, so data residency becomes a scheduler placement rule. Security is dual-use: the same accelerators train a hospital or a military model, so the US limits exports, and since July 2025 Malaysia needs a permit for US AI chips. Fairness has a compute cost too: Malay is under 0.1 percent of web text.

SLIDE 12, compute divide (0:25)
Frontier training compute grows about five times a year, and the best academic model sits about three thousand times lower. Most academics have one to eight GPUs. NCI Gadi has 776 GPUs and demand near three times its allocation. And if only companies can train frontier models, only companies can audit them.

SLIDE 13, SCALE (0:35)
UNESCO, the OECD and Malaysia's AIGE agree on values, but give operators no number to report. The EU is closer: the AI Act flags models above ten to the twenty-five FLOP, and sites over 500 kilowatts must report PUE. So we propose SCALE, where each step is a Slurm setting and a reported number. Size right: stop adding GPUs when parallel efficiency drops. Carbon-aware: jobs that can wait days and be paused cut CO2 by up to 19 percent. Account every joule. Keep data lawful and local. Give fair-share access.

SLIDE 14, limitations (0:28)
Our limits: a one-core CPU baseline on a shared Colab VM, so our speed-ups belong to that setup, and no GDS test. Most model energy figures are estimates, so we measured ours: about 0.7 joules per 8K image on the T4. Google's fleet only saved 1 to 2 percent at peak-carbon hours. Next, a GDS server and Slurm energy accounting. So: rotation is memory bound and PCIe sets the ceiling, and the cost of a FLOP depends on where and for whom it runs.
